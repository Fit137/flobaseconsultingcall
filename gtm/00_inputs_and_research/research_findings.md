## Step 2.1 — Competitor Profiles

### Radius (formerly Radiusbob)
- **What it is:** Lead management and CRM built specifically for the insurance industry, founded 2010 by Clu Connor.
- **Features named:** Sales automation for lead distribution, task assignment, text messaging, screen sharing, quote generation. Workflow automation triggered by lead source, tag, carrier, location and coverage type. Integrated VoIP with local and toll-free numbers, call recording, IVR. Prebuilt campaign bundles for life, final expense and mortgage protection.
- **Target audience:** Independent agents, telesales teams, small agencies.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Imports leads directly from lead vendors; integrates with other platforms; "fantastic" customer service; features added continuously.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** Email integration is lacking and requires multiple tools; no easy document attachment or video embedding. G2 sample is 1 review at 5.0, which G2 itself flags as insufficient for buying insight.
- **Pricing:** {TBD} publicly; historically low double-digit per user per month.

### AgencyBloc AMS+
- **What it is:** Positions as "the #1 Recommended Insurance Industry Growth Platform" for health, benefits and senior insurance.
- **Features named:** Client and policy management, commission processing, quoting, workflow automation, compliance tracking, full phone system, online scheduling, task management, email marketing, texting, commission statements, custom reporting.
- **Target audience:** Independent agencies, GAs, IMO/FMO and call centers; benefits and senior market weighted.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Responsive and knowledgeable support; strong client and policy organization; searchable policy, client and activity history; measurable productivity gains once configured.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** "Can feel a little clunky at first"; commission mapping and setup take significant time; onboarding gaps and implementation delays tied to integrations and system loading; price and the one-year commitment read as expensive next to alternatives.
- **Pricing:** From $109 per user per month (Grow tier), one-year commitment, three tiers (Grow, Accelerate, Elevate). Mass email quoted as an add-on at ~2 cents each.

### Convoso
- **What it is:** Cloud contact center and predictive dialer platform.
- **Features named:** Predictive dialing, list management, dynamic scripting, advanced reporting, caller ID reputation management.
- **Target audience:** Outbound call centers; **47% of Convoso reviewers are in insurance**, the single largest industry.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Intuitive interface that shortens training; measurable lift in contact rates and revenue attributed to the dialer and caller ID management; strong onboarding service; reachable support.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** Widely described as too expensive for what it delivers; no published pricing, so buyers must take a sales call to learn cost; frequent bugs, lag and occasional outages causing dropped calls; generally not viable under about 20 agents.
- **Pricing:** Custom only, per seat or per minute.

### Ricochet360
- **What it is:** "The All-in-One Auto Dialer, CRM/Lead Management, and Marketing Automation Platform that creates predictable revenue outcomes by automating 95% of your agent's daily sales workflow."
- **Features named:** Auto and predictive dialer, CRM, lead management, marketing automation across text, email and voicemail, internal chat, custom fields, call tracking, skills-based queues, drip email, gamification, scripting, reporting.
- **Target audience:** Small business, 99% of reviewers; **75% of reviewers are in insurance**; speed-to-lead use cases in insurance and mortgage.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Responsive, knowledgeable and patient support during onboarding and troubleshooting.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** Buyers report feeling misled into contracts; features presented as included turned out to be missing or cost extra, undisclosed upfront.
- **Pricing:** Booster $162 per user per month; Galaxy $210 per user per month; **$585 per month minimum spend**; onboarding fees additional.

### Agent CRM
- **What it is:** AI-powered all-in-one sales and marketing platform built for insurance agents, built on HighLevel, positioned as the fastest-growing platform for insurance agents.
- **Features named:** Lead scoring, predictive renewal risk, AI task prioritization, "Branning Bundles" of prebuilt per-vertical campaigns with pre-written texts and emails, follow-up sequences, pipeline automation, appointment reminders, no-show follow-up, referral requests, funnels, social tools.
- **Target audience:** Individual insurance agents and their teams.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Everything unlocked at one price; support staff who understand insurance products and compliance rather than generic platform support; 24/7 chat, email and phone plus a Facebook group and daily live Zoom calls; campaigns that revive dead leads.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** Inherits HighLevel's complexity, which is the single most common complaint about that platform; sub-account-under-a-reseller structure makes data, automations and numbers painful to migrate out; usage fees for SMS, calls and email land on top of subscription, with real HighLevel agency bills of $300 to $600 per month once wallet fees are included.
- **Pricing:** $97 per month after a 14-day trial, or $470 per year.

### EverQuote (Agent / Lead Connection Service)
- **What it is:** One of the largest US online insurance marketplaces, matching in-market shoppers to agents; sells data leads, calls and live transfers.
- **Features named:** Consumer-initiated live transfers, lead outreach team that warms and transfers, filters by risk profile, subsidy programs.
- **Target audience:** Established agencies with proven live-call conversion economics, high-LTV books and producer capacity for inbound flow.
- **Strengths from reviews:** Live transfer bind rates of 20 to 30 percent; filters produce good risks that stay on the books; account specialists rated highly.
- **Weaknesses from reviews:** Highest cost per call in the category by a wide margin at $50 to $100+ per call; the model assumes the agency can staff inbound and convert well enough to justify it; it is a lead source only, so it leaves CRM, dialing, hierarchy and reporting unsolved.
- **Pricing:** Varies by product, lead type and subsidy eligibility; $50 to $100+ per call for live transfers.

## Step 1.1 — Feature Analysis Research

Feature presence across the set, plus what customers praise and complain about, feeding Phase 1 demand and competitiveness scoring.

**Features customers praise most (across all six):** contact rate lift from dialing, prebuilt insurance campaign templates, insurance-literate support, direct lead-vendor import, searchable policy and activity history.

**Features customers complain about or wish existed:**
1. Opaque pricing and forced sales calls before seeing a number (Convoso, AgencyBloc, EverQuote).
2. Hidden or unbundled costs discovered after signature (Ricochet360, AgencyBloc add-ons, HighLevel wallet fees).
3. Minimum spends and annual commitments that punish small and seasonal agencies (Ricochet360 $585 per month floor, AgencyBloc one-year term).
4. Tool sprawl: the lead vendor, the dialer, the CRM and the reporting layer are four different vendors, and integrations between them break.
5. No line from lead spend to issued policy, so cost per acquisition per agent stays invisible.
6. Nothing in the category manages an agent hierarchy, downline recruiting or licensing and contracting progress. AgencyBloc serves IMO/FMO but through commissions and compliance, not through recruiting and ramp.

**Market economics that set the pricing anchors (Phase 9):**
- Final expense live transfers: $35 to $75 per transfer, 25 to 35% close rates for experienced producers, 15 to 20% in an agent's first six months.
- Broader live transfer range: $40 to $120+ per connected call. Life insurance live transfers $80 to $180.
- Worked example from the market: an $85 live transfer at 100% contact, 85% qualification and 20% close produces a ~$500 cost per close; at $110 per call and 15% close, cost per close exceeds $730.
- Aggregator channels carry bogus-lead rates above 50%, and close rates of 2 to 10% depending on source.
- Agency CPA math the category already uses: total lead spend divided by deals closed; profitable when CPA sits below average commission per sale.

**Talent economics that set the onboarding-tracker case:**
- More than 90% of new agents quit within a year; roughly 90% quit within three years; ~85% turnover industry-wide.
- First-year attrition splits roughly: 11% in the first three months, 19% between three and six months, 17% between seven and twelve months.
- Recruiters project $4,000 to $6,000 earned per agent hired, which is a fair proxy for what an owner sinks into each recruit.
- Life and Health licensing exam first-time pass rates run 55 to 70%; total time from start to licensed is 2 to 8 weeks; carrier appointments need 3 to 5 carriers minimum.

### Positive themes (4 and 5 star) — what competitors currently lead in

| Competitor | Theme 1 | Theme 2 | Theme 3 | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Convoso | Contact rate and connect volume from predictive dialing | Caller ID reputation management | Onboarding service quality | Dominant, repeated |
| Ricochet360 | Speed-to-lead automation across text, email, voicemail | Responsive support during onboarding | Workflow automation depth | Strong |
| AgencyBloc | Commission processing accuracy | Searchable policy, client and activity history | Knowledgeable, reachable support | Dominant, repeated |
| Radius | Direct lead-vendor import | Insurance-specific automation triggers | Personal customer service | Moderate, thin sample |
| Agent CRM | Prebuilt per-vertical campaign bundles | Insurance-literate support and daily live community | Everything unlocked at one price | Strong |
| EverQuote | Live transfer bind rates of 20 to 30% | Risk-quality filters | Account specialist support | Strong |

**Consolidated: the two things this category is currently good at are (1) dialing throughput and connect rate, and (2) depth of the policy and commission record.**

| "Too expensive for what it offers", no published price, forced sales call | Convoso, AgencyBloc, EverQuote | Spend-to-sale transparency |
| Hidden and undisclosed costs found after signing; features sold as included that were not | Ricochet360, AgencyBloc add-ons, HighLevel wallet fees | Spend-to-sale transparency |
| Minimum spend floors and annual lock-ins | Ricochet360 $585 per month, AgencyBloc 12-month term | Spend-to-sale transparency |
| Four vendors to run one sales floor; brittle integrations between them | Whole category | Single-tab agency operations |
