# Dashboard 5 — Buying Committee (Flobase)

Skill steps 5.1 and 5.2. Three persona levels per ICP, plus the design partner profile.

## ICP Level 1 Personas (MVP to PMF, 5 to 25 agent agency)

| Attribute | User | Manager | Decision Maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Possible Job Titles | Licensed Agent, Closer, Producer, Final Expense Agent, Telesales Agent | Sales Manager, Team Lead, Floor Manager, Director of Sales, Senior Agent | Agency Owner, Founder, Managing Partner, Agency Principal |
| Responsibility Area | Working assigned and purchased leads, taking inbound calls, submitting applications, following up on pending business | Daily call standards, lead distribution across the floor, coaching call recordings, holding agents to close-rate targets | Lead budget, carrier contracts, recruiting, agency profitability, everything nobody else owns |
| Purchasing Influence | Low on the decision, decisive on adoption. If the agent will not live in the tool, the tool is gone within a month | Medium. Recommends, pilots, and is the loudest voice if a tool slows the floor down | High and usually total. Signs, pays and implements. In this ICP the owner is the entire approval chain |
| Core Customer Journey Stages | Value Realization, Expansion and Advocacy | Vendor Evaluation, Trial and Purchase Decision, Value Realization | Problem Awareness, Solution Discovery, Vendor Evaluation, Trial and Purchase Decision, Renewal |
| Potential Reasons to Block | "The dialer is slower than what we had." Fear that per-agent spend tracking is really surveillance. Losing a personal lead list built in the old CRM. Another login to learn during a comp month | "Migration will cost us a selling week." Skepticism that inbound volume is real. Concern that new reporting will expose their team's numbers before they can fix them | Price with no published number to sanity-check. Concentration risk in one young vendor. Data portability if it fails. No recognizable customer to reference. The last platform that overpromised routing |
| Drivers | More conversations with people who actually want coverage, less admin between calls, faster commission because submissions do not get lost | A floor that hits its number without them chasing it, coachable recordings, one screen that shows who is behind before the week ends | Cost per issued policy below average first-year commission, budget that cannot leak, a book that is an asset, and a week off without the floor stalling |
| Assets to Get Buy-In | Sixty-second in-product walkthrough of the agent day, side-by-side dial-to-conversation comparison, an existing agent's recorded testimonial | Migration runbook with a named timeline, live inbound routing demo on their own numbers, a lead-distribution rules sheet | Agency Profit Leak Scorecard, stack cost comparison against published competitor list prices, funded wallet trial, data export policy in writing, unit economics one-pager |
| Channels to Find Them | Facebook agent groups, YouTube final expense channels, IMO Slack and Discord communities, agency-internal training calls | The same communities plus LinkedIn, and inside their own agency once the owner is in a trial | Facebook agency-owner groups, IMO and FMO conferences, YouTube agency-building channels, podcasts aimed at agency owners, cold email and LinkedIn, referral from another owner |

## ICP Level 2 Personas (Growth, 50 to 500 agents across sub-agencies)

| Attribute | User | Manager | Decision Maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Possible Job Titles | Licensed Agent, Producer inside a sub-agency, Downline Agent | Branch Owner, Sub-Agency Owner, Regional Sales Manager, Director of Agent Development, Recruiting Manager | IMO or FMO Principal, President, COO, Head of Distribution, VP of Operations |
| Responsibility Area | Same as Level 1, inside a branch. Also carries the branch's production standard | Branch production, recruiting and ramping new agents, allocating the branch's lead budget, holding override economics for their downline | Distribution strategy, carrier compensation tiers, total lead spend across branches, agency valuation, systems consolidation |
| Purchasing Influence | Low individually, high in aggregate. Branch-level adoption failure kills the rollout | High. Branch owners can veto by non-adoption and are usually consulted before a hierarchy-wide rollout | High, but shared. Needs an ops or finance counter-signature and increasingly a compliance opinion |
| Core Customer Journey Stages | Value Realization | Trial and Purchase Decision, Value Realization, Expansion and Advocacy | Problem Awareness, Solution Discovery, Vendor Evaluation, Renewal and Loyalty |
| Potential Reasons to Block | Loyalty to their branch's existing tools. Perception that head office now sees their personal numbers | "My branch's numbers will be compared to branches with better leads." Loss of discretion over lead allocation. Fear that transparency reduces their override leverage | Migration risk across many branches at once. No commission processing, so AgencyBloc or the existing AMS stays anyway. Vendor concentration. Security and compliance review. Contract and data-ownership terms across a hierarchy |
| Drivers | Enough good leads to hit personal AP targets, being paid on time, a clear path to running their own branch | Branch profitability they can defend in a leadership meeting, faster agent ramp, a recruiting pipeline that does not stall at licensing | Comparable branch economics, higher carrier compensation tiers on clean volume, fewer vendors, and a distribution asset that survives due diligence |
| Assets to Get Buy-In | In-product onboarding path, peer testimonial from another branch | Branch economics dashboard walkthrough, hierarchy and permissions map, ramp-time benchmark from a comparable branch | Multi-branch rollout plan with named phases, security and data-portability documentation, total stack cost model across all branches, executive business case with `{TBD}` current-state baseline, reference call with a comparable IMO |
| Channels to Find Them | Inside the hierarchy once rollout starts | IMO leadership meetings and annual conferences, private branch-owner communities, referral from the principal | Industry conferences, carrier and IMO relationships, direct outbound to named accounts, warm introductions from Level 1 owners who grew into a hierarchy, LinkedIn |

## Design Partner Profile

Early adopters with the affinity, risk tolerance and time to shape the product.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design Partner Attributes | Owner-operator of a 8 to 20 agent life telesales agency who is already running an unofficial version of Flobase in spreadsheets. Vocal in at least one agent community. Has switched CRM or dialer at least once in the past two years, so the migration objection is already answered for them. Willing to share real spend and AP numbers under NDA |
| Job Titles | Agency Owner, Founder, Managing Partner. Occasionally a Director of Sales with full budget delegation |
| Demographics | US-based, remote-first agency, typically 28 to 45, three to eight years in the industry, often a former top producer who built a downline rather than a career manager. Active on Facebook and YouTube, not on LinkedIn |
| Responsibility Areas | Lead purchasing decisions, agent recruiting, carrier contracting, weekly production review, payroll and advances |
| Current Tools | A dialer (Ricochet360, Convoso or a VoIP plus autodialer), a CRM (Radius, Agent CRM or a HighLevel sub-account), one to three lead vendors billed separately, and Google Sheets as the reconciliation layer. Occasionally an AMS for the book |
| Key Pain Points | Cannot compute cost per issued policy per agent. Wallet or lead spend leaks into agents who never produce. Reconciles three invoices by hand. Loses recruits between licensing exam and contracting. Rebuilds the same report every Monday |
| Critical Triggers | A month where lead spend rose and issued AP did not. An agent leaving with unreconciled spend. A contract renewal with a price increase or minimum spend. Crossing ten agents. Being pushed by their IMO to build a downline |
| Goals and Motivations | Hold $100k issued AP per month. Get CPA under average first-year commission. Build something that runs without them. Recruit and keep agents rather than churn through them |
| Decision Criteria | Does it show cost per issued policy per agent. Can it replace at least two current vendors. Can I get my data out. Is the inbound volume real. Is there a month-to-month option. Can my worst agent use it without training |
| Influence Level | Total within their own agency. Meaningful in their community: a design partner who posts a result in a Facebook agent group reaches hundreds of comparable owners in a day |
| Barriers to Purchase | No published pricing to sanity-check. No named references yet. Concentration risk of putting leads, CRM and money with one young vendor. Migration cost during a comp month. Skepticism earned from platforms that overpromised routing |
| Information Sources | Facebook agency-owner groups, YouTube agency-building and final expense channels, IMO leadership calls, podcasts for agency owners, peer text threads. Review sites matter much less here than in generic B2B SaaS |
| Success Metrics | Cost per issued policy per agent, issued AP per agent per month, wallet burn against issued AP, percentage of recruits reaching first sale within 90 days, number of vendor subscriptions cancelled |
| Early Adopter Characteristics | Tolerates rough edges in exchange for influence over the roadmap. Will run a parallel pilot on a subset of agents rather than demanding a full cutover. Answers a Sunday message. Wants to be named as a case study because it helps their own recruiting |

**Design partner terms to offer:** 5 to 8 partners, 90-day engagement, funded starter wallet, weekly 30-minute feedback call, roadmap influence in writing, locked founding pricing for 24 months, and a named case study on completion. Ask in return for real spend and AP data, one recorded testimonial, and one introduction to a comparable owner.
